# Orra privacy

English · [简体中文](privacy.zh-CN.md) · [日本語](privacy.ja.md) · [Español](privacy.es.md)

The [README](../README.md) has a short summary. This page has the details.

Orra turns your speech into text on your Mac and never sends your recordings or their text anywhere. It has no account, no analytics and no server of its own. The text it pastes stays on this Mac's clipboard, so Universal Clipboard does not offer it to your other devices. The same goes for text you copy with Copy Last Dictation or with the copy commands of Live Captions. A caption line you select and copy with Command C goes to the regular clipboard, as in any app.

**Learning from your corrections** is off until you turn it on. While it is on, Orra reads the text of the field it just pasted into, through macOS Accessibility, once a second for up to 3 minutes after each paste, and only while you are in that field. It reads at most 20,000 characters, keeps the text in memory during those minutes only, and never writes it anywhere. It never reads password fields, an app with secure input on or Orra's own windows. It keeps only the word pairs, such as 奥拉 and Orra, with how often and when each was seen, in ~/Library/Application Support/io.github.db-ol.Orra/corrections.json, and forgets a pair seen once after a week. Runs of three or more digits, such as codes, amounts and years, are never kept. Orra changes dictated text only where a kept pair's misheard spelling appears. Forget Learned Corrections in Settings deletes all pairs.

**Use names on screen** is off until you turn it on. While it is on, each time you start a dictation, Orra reads through macOS Accessibility up to 1,000 characters before the cursor in the field you dictate into, and the title of its window. In an app that cannot give part of a field, Orra reads the whole field when it holds 20,000 characters or fewer and keeps only the last 1,000 characters before the cursor. A field that does not say where its cursor is gives only the window title. It never reads password fields, password managers (1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC, Apple's Passwords and Keychain Access) or an app with secure input on, and never Orra's own windows. It takes up to 40 names and rare words from that text and gives them to the speech model on your Mac, never the text itself. It keeps the text and the names in memory for that dictation only, and never saves or logs them.

**Meeting Interpretation** keeps captions and the words said in the voice sample in memory only. Captions stay there until Orra quits, so you can copy them after a meeting. It keeps the voice sample in memory too, unless you turn on Remember my voice, which is off until you do. It never logs text or audio.

**Projects** is a preview, off until you turn it on in Settings, Projects.

- **What it reads.** The mail of the Mail mailboxes you choose in Settings, Projects and of any mailbox you link to a project, received within the period you choose, 90 days unless you change it, and the files in the folders you add to a project. Until you choose, Inbox and Sent of all accounts are ticked. No mail is read before you click Start Reading Mail. Before that, Orra reads only the names of your accounts and mailboxes in Mail, so Settings, Projects can list them for you to choose from. Orra reads mail through Apple Events, and macOS asks you to allow them only when you click Allow Mail Access… in Settings, Projects. It reads only while Mail is open, never opens Mail itself and never reads Mail's files on disk. While it reads a mailbox back through that period, as after Start Reading Mail, the Projects window says Reading mail… with the number of emails read so far. A change of the mailboxes or the period counts for the mail it reads from then on, and what it saved before stays until it expires or you delete it. It also reads the names and addresses of your accounts in Mail, to tell the mail you sent, and Explain Message Selected in Mail asks Mail which message you selected there. From the mail it reads, it takes the text of attachments in PDF, Word, RTF, OpenDocument, Markdown, plain text, CSV and calendar files, up to 10 MB each and 64 KB of text from each. In your folders it skips files that are not on this Mac, such as iCloud Drive files that are not downloaded, so macOS never downloads anything for it. It never reads your home folder, a whole disk or Mail's local data files, even when you add one.
- **What it does with it.** Rules in Orra sort each new email into a project, and while the Projects window is open, the cleanup model on your Mac sorts what the rules leave open and names the projects Orra suggests. When you click Explain, the model explains the email with sources from the project's files, its attachments and earlier mail, and drafts a reply. The model has no tools. It cannot open a link, run anything or act in Mail. Orra keeps from the model the text it finds hidden from the reader in an email, an attachment or a file, and the lines it recognizes there as speaking to an assistant, and the window warns you about them. Orra sends nothing you read, write or say in Projects anywhere.
- **What it never does.** It never sends, moves, deletes or forwards mail, and reading a message never marks it as read. There is no Send button. Open Reply in Mail asks Mail to open its reply window and puts the draft on this Mac's clipboard for you to paste, kept from Universal Clipboard and marked for clipboard managers to skip, as dictated text is. It shows mail and what the model wrote as plain text only, with no HTML, no images and no link that opens, so reading mail in Orra loads nothing from the internet. It never writes, moves or deletes anything in your folders.
- **What it keeps**, in ~/Library/Application Support/io.github.db-ol.Orra/Projects, where only you can open the folder and read its files. Your projects, with their names, descriptions, folders, people, domains, rules and linked mailboxes, are in projects.json, with a hash of the domain, address, mailbox or subject word behind each suggested project you dismissed. Time Machine backs it up like your other files. Everything else is in its Index folder, left out of backups: the text of the files in your project folders, until a file leaves the project's folders or you forget the project. The text of each email and of its attachments, with its explanation, for 90 days after Orra read it. An explanation keeps the excerpts of project files it gave the model, with where each file was, as long as the explanation, also after a file leaves the project's folders. The rest of an email, such as its sender, subject, project, marks and warnings, for 180 days after Orra read the newest email of its conversation. Drafts, with the text you typed into them and the changes you asked for, for 30 days after their last change, your typing included, but never longer than the email's text. When a draft goes, its explanation stays as long as the email's text. Counts of the emails you assigned to a project yourself, by the other side's address and domain, for 180 days after the last assignment. A hash of the Message-ID of removed mail, so it is not read again, for up to 365 days. Orra removes what is older when Projects starts and once a day while it is on, as Settings, Projects says under Data. What you keep while Projects is off stays until you turn it on again or delete it. What it deletes there is overwritten in the database. Your choice of mailboxes and period is in Orra's preferences.
- **Deleting it.** Forget Project… in the Projects window deletes one project with its index, mail and drafts. Delete All Projects Data… in Settings, Projects deletes everything Projects keeps, your choice of mailboxes too, and turning Projects off asks whether to keep it or delete it. Your mail in Mail and the files in your folders stay as they are. Assigning, editing and deleting in Projects change only what it keeps on this Mac, never your mail in Mail.
- **Logs** hold counts, timings and error codes only, never mail or file text, a subject, an address, a name or a file name.
- **Network.** Orra itself makes no network request for Projects, other than the cleanup model download you agree to. Orra never connects to a mail server. The Mail app fetches your mail as it always does, and when Projects asks for a message whose text Mail has not downloaded yet, Mail may fetch it from your mail server, as it does when you open that message. The reply window that Open Reply in Mail opens is Mail's own, and Mail may save it to your account's Drafts as it does any reply.

**Orra uses the internet for model downloads you ask for and for update checks only.**

- **The speech model**, only after you choose Download Speech Model, or Download Models in the welcome window: six files, 2.47 GB, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co). If Hugging Face cannot be reached, for example from mainland China, Orra tries ModelScope (modelscope.cn) and then hf-mirror.com, which carry the same files. Orra checks the size and SHA-256 hash of every file against values in its source code, so a mirror cannot change the model. The files live in ~/Library/Application Support/io.github.db-ol.Orra/Models, left out of Time Machine backups.
- **The cleanup model**, only after you click Download Models or Download Cleanup Model in the welcome window, which offers them to a new user on a Mac with 16 GB of memory or more or while cleanup is on, or turn on Clean Up Text, Translation or Projects in Settings and agree to its download, or click Download or Resume Download in the Live Captions window while it shows Translated or Both and agree to its download (or click Try Again there after a download failed): four files of mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-4bit, 3.05 GB, from the same servers, checked the same way, into its own folder under Models. A download you agree to in Live Captions turns on neither Clean Up Text nor Translation. It stops if you turn captions off before it ends, unless Clean Up Text, Translation or Projects needs the model by then.
- **The voice model**, only after you start its download for Speak Translations to Others: Qwen3-TTS, about 2 GB, from Hugging Face or hf-mirror.com, checked the same way.
- **Update checks**, only after you allow them. At its second launch Orra asks whether to check automatically, and Settings, About has the choice. A check reads https://github.com/db-ol/Orra/releases/latest/download/appcast.xml about once a day. Orra installs an update only when you choose it, each time, and only when the update and its feed are signed with the project's update key.

The models run on your Mac and never go online. Launching Orra never connects to the network until you allowed update checks. Like any download, these services see your IP address, and an update check also shows Orra's version and, as every web request does, your preferred languages. Orra sends no account, token or cookie, and nothing else about your Mac. [docs/model-download.md](model-download.md) describes the servers and the checks in detail.

**Report a Problem** writes a text file on your Mac with Orra's version, settings and permissions, the Mac's model, chip, memory, macOS version and languages, the microphone's name, Orra's own log from the last hour and its crash reports from the last 7 days. It holds no dictated text, audio, clipboard, vocabulary or learned words. Of Projects it says only whether Projects is on and how many projects, emails and files it holds. Your home folder, account name, full name and computer name are taken out. Orra sends nothing itself. It opens the GitHub page in your browser, and the file reaches GitHub only if you attach it. What you write yourself goes into a public issue, so for Projects leave out mail text, names, email addresses and screenshots of mail.

To remove the models, quit Orra and delete the Models folder above. To remove only the cleanup model, turn cleanup and translation off first and delete its folder, Qwen3.5-4B-4bit-0e7ffd5c, inside it. If ~/Library/Caches/qwen3-speech holds a copy from another app, delete that too, or Orra installs the model again from it.
