Live Captions can show only what was said, have their own language and a shortcut, and keep the whole meeting for you to copy. Settings has new Dictation and Translation pages.

- At the top of the caption window, or under Captions show in Settings, Meeting Interpretation, you choose Original, Translated or Both. Original shows what was said without translating it, so it never loads or downloads the local model. Translated and Both are the two earlier modes under new names, and your choice stays.
- Live Captions have their own language, Translate captions into, at the top of the caption window and in Settings, Meeting Interpretation. It offers English and your dictation language, and Translate into no longer changes captions. A sentence already in that language shows as it was said, and every other one is translated into it. A sentence in neither language, such as Japanese while you dictate in Chinese, goes into English. After the update it is the language you fixed under Translate into, or with Automatic your dictation language. So with Automatic, a meeting in your dictation language now shows as it was said instead of in English. That includes a Chinese meeting when you read English but dictate in 中文（普通话和粤语）+ English. Choose English in the caption window to have it translated again.
- Copy All Captions, at the top right of the caption window, copies every sentence the way the window shows it, and its arrow offers the originals, the translations or both. Captions now stay in memory until you quit Orra, so Copy Captions in Orra's menu still copies them after you turn them off. Turning captions on again starts an empty window and keeps the last meeting under Copy Previous Captions. Like Copy Last Dictation, these copies stay on this Mac, now also when a dictation puts one back on the clipboard after its paste.
- The Live Captions shortcut, Control Option Command C (⌃ ⌥ ⌘ C) on any keyboard layout, turns captions on and off from any app. Change it, or turn it off with None, in Settings, General, Keys. If your talk key or translation key is that combination, or a combination of only its modifiers, such as Control and Option, the shortcut starts as None.
- The new Dictation page in Settings holds Dictation language, Clean Up Text with its rules, and, under Recognition, Learn from my corrections and Use names on screen (Beta). The Translation page holds the Translation switch, the Translation key row with Change…, and Translate into. The keys stay on General, with On screen hint, the sounds, Open at Login and Show Orra in the Dock under System. Double press for hands free is now Double press in Hold to keep recording, under Keys. It shows only while a key is in Hold, and the Talk Key menu no longer has it. Every setting that moved keeps its value.
- If you never turned Translation on, the translation key now starts in Press to start and stop. A tap of it alone starts a recording, so press it again or Escape to end one you did not mean. Option shortcuts still work. If you turned Translation on before, the key keeps its mode.
- Once Orra is set up, opening it shows Settings, unless Open at Login is on.
- A new install starts with your own language as the dictation language, such as 日本語 + English (Beta) on a Mac with English first and Japanese second. During setup it follows the language you pick for Orra in the welcome window, until you pick a dictation language. A dictation language you already have stays.
- When cleanup or translation runs past its time limit, Orra now pastes without it at once, instead of waiting up to a few seconds for the model to stop.
- Learn from my corrections no longer reads Orra's own windows, such as Settings.
