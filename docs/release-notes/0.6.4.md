This update lets Live Captions show what you say into your microphone, keeps the screen on while captions are in view, and fixes a mistake in learning from corrections.

- To caption what you say, choose Microphone in the menu at the top left of the caption window, or under Captions listen to in Settings, Meeting Interpretation. Every launch starts with What the Mac Plays, so captions use the microphone only once you choose it. They use the microphone dictation uses, and the window shows Microphone on with its name.
- Captions let go of the microphone at once when you turn them off, close their window or choose What the Mac Plays. While the screen is locked or another user is using the Mac, they let go of it too, and start it again once you unlock or are back. Captions pause while you dictate, so what you dictate never becomes a caption.
- While Live Captions listen and their window is in view, the screen no longer dims or turns off on its own, whichever source they listen to. It dims and turns off as usual once captions turn off, their window closes or is out of view, or the screen locks, and after about 5 minutes without sound, as after a call. You can still lock the screen or close the lid at any time.
- With Learn from my corrections on, a change into a word with a Chinese numeral, such as 开会 to 十点, or into a single letter, such as plan A to plan B, could be learned as a misheard word even when the two sound different. Now it is learned only when the words sound alike, as any other change is. Changing one character into a numeral that sounds different, such as 两点 to 三点, no longer asks to add a word to your vocabulary.
