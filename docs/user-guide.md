# Orra user guide

English · [简体中文](user-guide.zh-CN.md) · [日本語](user-guide.ja.md) · [Español](user-guide.es.md)

The [README](../README.md) has the overview of Orra and the download, and [privacy.md](privacy.md) the privacy details.

## Getting started

1. Open Orra. A welcome window, with a small guide that says what to do next, walks you through four steps. At the top, pick Orra's language, English, 简体中文, 日本語 or Español, or keep the Mac's. The window switches to it right away. Below it, pick the language you dictate in, see Dictation language below. Until you pick one, it changes with the language you pick above. You can change Orra's language later in Settings, General, and the dictation language in Settings, Dictation. The first three steps make Orra ready, and the last one is up to you:
   - **Download the models**, once. On a Mac with 16 GB of memory or more, Download Models (5.52 GB) fetches the speech model, 2.47 GB, and then a second local model that cleans up what you say, 3.05 GB, behind one progress bar, and turns cleanup on. You can dictate as soon as the speech model is in place. The cleanup model needs about 4 GB of memory while it runs, and Clean up with a local model in Settings, Dictation turns cleanup off. On a smaller Mac this step downloads only the speech model, and you can turn on cleanup later in Settings, Dictation.
   - **Allow the microphone.**
   - **Allow Accessibility**, which Orra needs to notice the talk key and to paste the text. Once System Settings opens, a small card beside it shows in an animation where to turn Orra on. The card goes away when Orra is on. If you denied the microphone before, Open Microphone Settings… in the microphone step shows a similar card beside System Settings.
   - **Choose your talk key** by pressing it. On a Mac's own keyboard, fn (Globe) at the bottom left is comfortable while your right hand is on the mouse. On an external keyboard, right Control works best, because some, such as the Logitech MX Keys, never send fn. If you switch between the two, add the other key as a second talk key in Settings, see Keys below. Under the key, choose how it works: Press to start and stop, which a new user starts with, Hold or Double press.
2. Press the talk key, right Control unless you pick another, and speak. Press it again, and the text appears where your cursor is. With Hold, keep the key down while you speak and let go instead, and with Double press, press it twice quickly to start. Orra plays its start sound once the microphone delivers sound, so everything you say after the sound is recorded. While you speak, white bars in a small dark capsule at the bottom of the screen move with your voice (unless you turn this off in Settings), and macOS shows its orange microphone indicator in the menu bar.
3. Orra lives in the menu bar and the Dock. Its menu and Settings hold everything else. Once Orra is ready, the welcome window shows Try next: cleanup, translation and Meeting Interpretation, and on a Mac with 16 GB of memory or more, Projects, with a button that opens its page in Settings for each one that is not on yet. To open the window again, choose Setup Guide… in Orra's menu until Orra is ready, or Getting Started… once it is. When Orra is ready, opening it or clicking its Dock icon shows Settings. While Open at Login in Settings, General, System is on, Orra opens without a window, at login and also when you open it yourself. A click on its Dock icon still shows Settings.

## Using Orra

- **Keys.** Settings, General, Keys holds the talk key and the translation key. Click a key's field (or Change…) and press what you want: fn (Globe), the left or right Control, Option or Command key, or a combination such as Control and Option held together, or Shift Command D. Keyboards label keys differently, so the field shows the Mac name and symbol and, for Option and Command, what a Windows layout keyboard prints there, such as “⌥ Right Option (Alt)” or “⌘ Right Command (Windows key)”. Talk Key in the menu shows the keys and opens Settings at Keys to change them. Each key has a mode: Hold, Press to start and stop, or Double press. A new user's talk key starts in Press to start and stop. A talk key set up with an earlier version stays in Hold until you pick another mode. The translation key starts in Press to start and stop too, unless you turned Translation on in an earlier version. Then it stays in Hold until you pick another mode. In Hold, a left Control, Option or Command key starts only after you hold it alone for a moment, and in Press to start and stop a quick press of it starts at its release. Either way its shortcuts keep working. Shift, Caps Lock and keys that type cannot be a key on their own, and shortcuts that macOS or most apps use, such as Command C or Control Space, cannot be chosen. The field says why and which keys work. The regular key of a combination never reaches the app in front, and pressed alone it types as usual. Second key, right under the talk key, adds one more single key that works just like the talk key, in its mode, such as right Control beside fn. That helps when you switch to an external keyboard that never sends fn. With fn as the talk key and no second key, Orra offers one once, when an external keyboard is connected. If an earlier version had several talk keys on, Orra keeps one: fn first, then right Control, then the other right hand keys, then the left hand ones. Live Captions shortcut, last under Keys, turns Live Captions on and off from any app, with no permission needed. It starts as Control Option Command C (⌃ ⌥ ⌘ C) on any keyboard layout. If, when Orra added it, your talk key or translation key was that combination, or a combination of only its modifiers, such as Control and Option, it starts as None instead. To change it, click its field and press a key with Control or Command, or a function key such as F13. Option alone or with Shift does not work, since macOS refuses it. None turns it off. If macOS refuses the shortcut, for example because another app already holds it, Settings says so. It cannot be one of Orra's keys, and they cannot be it. While a key is a combination of only modifiers that are in the shortcut, such as Control and Option, pressing the shortcut may start a dictation for a moment, and Settings says so. The same happens when the talk key is a right hand key, such as right Control, and you press it first, so press the shortcut with your left hand.
- **Hands free.** With a key in Hold, press it twice quickly, then let go and keep talking. The indicator says which key finishes. Press it once to paste, or press Escape to cancel. A recording stops after 5 minutes, see Long dictations. Double press in Hold to keep recording, under Keys in Settings, General, turns it off. That switch shows only while a key is in Hold.
- **Long dictations.** A recording stops after 5 minutes, however you started it. In the last 15 seconds the indicator counts down, and after the stop it says why. A recording longer than a minute is cut at pauses into pieces of up to a minute, which Orra transcribes one after another once you let go. On an M5 Pro that takes about 2 seconds per minute of speech, and up to twice as long in Chinese with a vocabulary. If a piece cannot be transcribed, Orra pastes the rest and says so. Cleanup and translation leave out dictations over 400 and 300 characters, and Orra says when it pasted one without them. In hands free, Press to start and stop and Double press, the press you meant to stop with may come after the limit stopped the recording. So until the message goes, 30 seconds after it shows, the next press of that key only clears the message and starts no recording. With a key in Hold, holding it still records as usual, and a quick double press still starts hands free.
- **Vocabulary.** In Settings, Vocabulary, add the words and names you use. Orra gives the speech model the 200 you added or used most recently with every dictation, since a longer list helps less per word and slows every dictation. On test speech this raised the share of such terms written correctly from about 84% to 99% in Chinese and from 83% to 96% in English, and listed words were almost never inserted when nobody said them. The list holds up to 5,000 words, a cap only against runaway data. The table shows for each word whether you added it or Orra learned it, how Orra misheard it, and when you last used it.
- **Learning from your corrections.** Turn on “Learn from my corrections” in Settings, Dictation, under Recognition. When you fix a misheard word right after a dictation, Orra adds your spelling to the vocabulary and shows a notice with Undo. That includes a space taken out or put in, such as read me to readme. When you fix one Chinese character of a name, such as 林悦 to 霖悦, Orra asks instead and offers its guess of the whole word to edit. Later, where the speech model still writes the misheard spelling, Orra writes your word, as long as learning is on and the word is in your vocabulary. A misheard spelling made only of ordinary words, such as there for their, is never replaced, and putting the misheard spelling back stops the replacement. A word you remove is learned again the next time you fix it, while a word you undid is not. Learning works in apps that let macOS read their text, such as Notes, Mail and Safari. In other apps, copy the word and choose the add item in Orra's menu.
- **Names on screen (Beta).** Off until you turn on “Use names on screen (Beta)” in Settings, Dictation, under Recognition. Each time you start a dictation, Orra reads up to 1,000 characters before the cursor in the field you dictate into and the title of its window, takes up to 40 names and rare words from them, such as Quorvia, 甄若岚 or gRPC, and gives them to the speech model ahead of your vocabulary, so a name written there comes out spelled the same way. The text itself never goes to the model. On the clips of a test on 2026-10-15 and 16, where the name said was on the screen, 161 of 201 names came out right instead of 107, and 70 of 81 instead of 59 on recorded human speech, counted as that test counts, while the other words did not get worse. The known risk: when a name on screen sounds like a different name you say, Orra can write the name on screen instead. On recorded human speech this changed 13 of 59 names that came out right without it, which is why it is off by default. It works in apps that let macOS read their text, never in a password field or a password manager. In a chat or a reply to an email, the conversation and the quoted mail are not before the cursor, so there it uses only what you typed and the window title. When a list of names comes out in place of what you said, Orra pastes what the speech model heard without the names.
- **Fillers and numbers.** Before it pastes, Orra removes fillers that only fill a pause, such as 呃, 额, um and uh, and keeps words that carry meaning, such as 好啊, 吧 and 那个. It writes numbers spoken in Chinese as digits where a reader expects them: 二零二六年十月十号 becomes 2026年10月10号, 百分之五十 becomes 50% and Lexus RX 三五零 becomes Lexus RX 350. Small counts such as 三本书, rough numbers and idioms stay in words. Each has its switch in Settings, Dictation, under Clean Up Text.
- **Cleanup with a local model.** For a new user on a Mac with 16 GB of memory or more, Download Models in the welcome window also downloads its model, Qwen3.5 4B, 3.05 GB, and turns cleanup on. Otherwise it is off until you turn on Clean up with a local model in Settings, Dictation, and Orra then asks before it downloads the model. The same switch turns it off. Orra runs the model on your Mac after the filler and number rules. It removes fillers the rules leave, such as 那个 and 就是说 used as fillers, and applies spoken self corrections, such as 三点，哦不对，四点 or “Monday, I mean Tuesday”. Loaded, it needs about 4 GB of memory. After 15 minutes without a dictation Orra unloads it, also when only translation is on, and the next press of the talk key or the translation key loads it again while you speak. On an M5 Pro it added 0.6 to 1 second to a short dictation and 3 to 6 seconds to one of 400 Chinese characters, and slower Macs take longer. Dictations longer than 400 characters are pasted without cleanup, and when such a dictation has fillers or a correction, Orra says so. Orra holds back a cleanup that looks like an answer or a carried out request, or that adds words or numbers, and pastes the text without it, as it does when the model takes too long. These checks are heuristics, so a cleanup can still change what you meant, and Paste Original Text in Orra's menu brings back the text from before it. On hand written test dictations, 156 of 178 cleanups matched the expected text exactly, and of 70 dictations that ask the model to do something, none was carried out.
- **Translation.** Off until you turn on Translation in Settings, Translation. The translation key is under Keys in Settings, General, and works once translation is on. It uses the cleanup model, also while cleanup is off, and asks to download it first when it is not on your Mac. Press the translation key, right Option unless you pick another (right Command when right Option is your talk key), speak, and press it again. With Hold, keep it down while you speak and let go instead. Orra pastes your words translated between English and your dictation language: Chinese into English and English into Chinese, and the same with Japanese or Spanish when you dictate in them. With Japanese or Spanish, text in another script, such as Korean, goes into English. Under Translate into you can have everything pasted in English or in your dictation language instead. A choice made there for another dictation language stays saved for when you go back to it, and works as Automatic meanwhile. Into Chinese, Japanese or Spanish, common English terms such as PR or iPhone may stay in English. The translation key counts as pressed only after 0.3 seconds, or at its release when that comes sooner in Press to start and stop, so Option shortcuts and the characters right Option types keep working. As a right hand key or fn, it starts the microphone as you press it, and the start sound plays once the press counts and the microphone delivers sound, so what you say after the sound is recorded. A shortcut within the 0.3 seconds drops the recording right away, though macOS may show its orange microphone indicator for a moment. With Hold, press it twice quickly to translate hands free. In Press to start and stop, a tap of the key on its own starts a recording that goes on until you press the key again, so press Escape to cancel one you did not mean. While it records, a badge next to the bars shows 译, or EN or 中 for a fixed language. With Japanese it shows 訳, EN or 日, and with Spanish ⇄, EN or ES. Picking one key's key for the other swaps the two, and Settings says so. Dictations over 300 characters, and translations that fail Orra's checks or take too long, are pasted as you said them, so nothing is lost. Paste Original Text brings back the text from before the translation, Copy Last Dictation copies the translation, and Orra never learns corrections from a translated paste. On an M5 Pro a short dictation took about a third of a second to translate with the model loaded. After the model was unloaded, a very short dictation may wait for it to load.
- **Meeting Interpretation.** New in 0.4.0. Its switches are on the Meeting Interpretation page of Settings, and under the Meeting Interpretation heading in Orra's menu, where Live Captions turns on with one click.
  - **Live Captions** is off at every launch. It captures the sound the Mac plays, never the microphone, and shows each sentence in a floating window. At the top of the window, or in Settings, choose what it shows: Original, Translated or Both. Translated and Both translate each sentence with the local model. Original shows what was said without translating it, so it never loads or downloads the local model, and sentences heard while it is chosen stay untranslated after a switch. About a second after someone starts to talk, their first words show in small grey type and grow while they speak. When the sentence ends, its final text takes their place and the translation follows. Captions have their own language, Translate captions into, next to Original, Translated and Both at the top of the window, and in Settings, Meeting Interpretation. Translate into of the translation key does not change it. It offers English and your dictation language. It starts as your own language: the language Orra shows when that is Chinese, Japanese or Spanish, otherwise the first of them in the Mac's languages, or English with none of them. When that is neither English nor your dictation language, captions use your dictation language. If you set Orra up with an earlier version, it starts as your fixed choice under Translate into, or otherwise as your dictation language. A sentence already in the chosen language shows as it was said, so a meeting in your dictation language is no longer translated into English unless you choose English. Every other sentence is translated into it. A sentence in neither language, such as Japanese while you dictate in Chinese or Chinese while you dictate in Japanese, goes into English. A choice made for another dictation language stays saved for when you go back to it, and captions use your dictation language meanwhile. A new choice counts from the next sentence. In Original the choice is off, since Original does not translate. macOS asks once to allow Orra under Screen & System Audio Recording. Without the local model the window shows what was said until you download it. Closing the window turns captions off. The Live Captions shortcut, Control Option Command C unless you change it in Settings, General, Keys, turns captions on and off from any app. Captions stay in memory only, and are kept until you quit Orra, so you can still copy them. Copy All Captions, the button at the top right of the window, copies every sentence the way the window shows it. Its arrow opens a menu with Copy Original Text, Copy Translation and Copy Original and Translation. Lines still being heard are left out. After the window is closed, Copy Captions in the Orra menu copies them too. Turning captions on again starts an empty window and keeps the last meeting under Copy Previous Captions. Like Copy Last Dictation, these copies stay on this Mac's clipboard.
  - **Speak Translations to Others** needs Translation on, in Settings, Translation. A translated dictation of the translation key is also spoken by a voice model on your Mac, Qwen3-TTS, about 2 GB to download, into an output device: the one you pick, or Orra Microphone when it is installed, never the speakers on its own. Choose Orra Microphone as the microphone in the call app, and the other side hears the translation. Without Orra Microphone, pick a device such as a loopback device a call app can use as its microphone. Orra speaks one translation after another and waits while you dictate. It speaks translations into English, Chinese, Japanese and Spanish, and the script to read for a voice sample is in English or your dictation language. CosyVoice3, which some builds offer under Voice model, does not speak Japanese, so with it Orra says so instead of playing a Japanese translation, and pastes it even with Also paste the translated text off. By default the translation is pasted too. With a voice sample, each sentence is checked before it plays. To have it speak in your voice, click Record… beside Voice sample and read a short script aloud, about 10 seconds, or import an audio file there. Orra keeps the sample in memory until it quits. With Remember my voice on, which is off until you turn it on, Orra saves the sample's audio, never the words, in ~/Library/Application Support/io.github.db-ol.Orra/Voice, left out of backups, until you remove it or turn the switch off. After Orra restarts, it loads the sample once Speak Translations to Others is on. Use only your own voice, and tell the other side they hear a synthesized voice. Orra Microphone is a separate audio driver, not part of Orra.dmg. Download [OrraMicrophone.pkg](https://github.com/db-ol/Orra/releases/download/v0.4.0/OrraMicrophone.pkg) from the 0.4.0 release, signed and notarized, and open it to install. [Drivers/OrraMicrophone](../Drivers/OrraMicrophone/README.md) explains how to remove it or build it yourself.
- **Dictation language.** Dictation language in Settings, Dictation picks the language you speak besides English: 中文（普通话和粤语）+ English, 日本語 + English or Español + English, the last two as a beta. You can mix the two languages in one sentence. English is always one of them, and there is no third. The welcome window has the same choice. For a new user it starts as your own language. That is the language Orra shows when it is 简体中文, 日本語 or Español, and otherwise the first Chinese, Japanese or Spanish in the Mac's languages. So a Mac with English first and Japanese second starts with 日本語 + English, and so does a Japanese Mac with Orra set to English. With none of them it starts as 中文 + English. The first time you set Orra up, it follows the language you pick for Orra in the welcome window, until you pick a dictation language. After that it never changes by itself, also when you change Orra's language. If you set Orra up with an earlier version, it stays 中文 + English until you pick another.
  - **Chinese** means Mandarin and Cantonese. Cantonese comes out in simplified characters and keeps its own words, such as 嘅 and 咗, while 係 becomes 系. On public Cantonese recordings 4.8% to 5.7% of the characters were wrong, about as many as for Mandarin. The filler rule keeps the Cantonese verb 呃 (to cheat). The number rule, cleanup and translation were made for Mandarin and are not tested on Cantonese, and Cantonese mixed with English is not measured.
  - **Japanese or Spanish.** A sentence in that language is pasted as the speech model wrote it, and Japanese keeps its kanji, such as 東京駅. The filler and number rules, cleanup and learning from your corrections were made for Chinese and English, so they leave such a sentence alone, and a translation of it starts from the text as heard. The translation key translates such a sentence into English, even Japanese in kana only, and English into that language. With Translate into fixed to that language, a sentence already in it is pasted as you said it. English sentences get all of them as before. When the speech model writes another language, such as Chinese for Spanish speech, Orra transcribes the recording again in your language and then in English. Pick Japanese or Spanish only if you dictate in it. With Japanese, a sentence in kanji only counts as Japanese, since kanji alone cannot tell Japanese from Chinese, so Chinese speech skips the steps made for Chinese. With Spanish, Chinese speech counts as a mistake of the speech model and mostly comes out translated into Spanish. On read test speech 3.0% of the words were wrong in Spanish and 6.3% of the characters in Japanese, and the median sentence took 0.4 s. With 中文 + English, Orra takes the same speech for a mistake of the speech model and transcribes it again: the median sentence took 1.2 s and 1.1 s, 12.4% and 15.4% came out wrong, and a Chinese or English translation was pasted for 6% of the Spanish and 11% of the Japanese sentences.
  - Live Captions read each sentence by your dictation language too, from the next sentence on, and offer it and English under Translate captions into.
- **Microphone.** Orra records from the system's default input unless you choose another under Microphone. With the lid of a MacBook closed, its own microphone is off, so choose another one, such as a webcam's.
- **Sounds, on screen hint and Dock icon.** Settings, General, System turns the sounds off and sets the on screen hint: Always shows the bars while you dictate and a small bar while Orra is ready, Only while dictating leaves out the small bar, and Off shows neither. Show Orra in the Dock, in the same place, turns the Dock icon off.
- **Language.** Orra's menus and windows are in English, Simplified Chinese, Japanese and Spanish, following your Mac. Spanish is Latin American Spanish, also on a Mac in Spain's. To choose another, use Language in the welcome window, or Interface language in Settings, General, System, and restart Orra. After a choice in the welcome window, Orra restarts on its own when you click Done, unless Settings or another Orra window is still open or a model is still downloading.
- **Updates.** Choose Check for Updates in the menu, or let Orra check by itself in Settings, About, which also shows the version.
- **Reporting a problem.** Choose Report a Problem… in Orra's menu and describe what happened. Continue on GitHub opens a new issue with your text, Orra's version, macOS and your Mac model filled in. GitHub needs a free account, and issues there are public. Orra also writes a diagnostic report for you to drag into the issue. For a problem in Projects, describe the steps and leave out mail text, names, email addresses and screenshots of mail. The issue form's Area field offers Projects (Preview).

## Projects (Preview)

Projects is an optional part of Orra for your mail and your project folders, and a preview that may still change. It reads the mail of the Mail mailboxes you choose and the files in the folders you add to a project. It sorts each new email into its project, and when you ask, it explains an email with sources from your projects and drafts a reply. It works with the cleanup model on your Mac, needs a Mac with 16 GB of memory or more, and never sends, moves or deletes mail. [privacy.md](privacy.md) lists what it reads and keeps.

- **Turning it on.** Turn on Projects in Settings, Projects. It works with the Mail app on this Mac, so check first that your mail shows in Mail. If the cleanup model is not on your Mac yet, Orra asks before it downloads it, 3.05 GB. If the model is on your Mac but could not be loaded, the page says Could not be loaded, and Try Loading Again loads it again. Turning Projects on reads nothing yet. Under the switch, Set Up Projects lists the steps, each with a check mark once it is done: download the cleanup model, which goes on while you do the next steps, open the Mail app, allow Mail access, choose mailboxes and start reading, and, if you like, make your first project. You also find Projects in Try next in the welcome window, and in Orra's menu as Set Up Projects… while Projects is off. How to Use Projects, beside Privacy details, opens this guide.
- **Mail access.** Open Mail opens the Mail app. Orra opens Mail only when you click Open Mail. Allow Mail Access… works once Mail is open. Orra says what it reads, and after Continue, macOS asks whether Orra may control Mail. Click Allow. Projects reads mail only while Mail is open. If you clicked Don't Allow, the page says so, and Open Automation Settings… leads to Privacy & Security, Automation, where you turn on Mail under Orra. When you open Orra again with Mail closed, Orra cannot tell yet whether it may still read Mail, so the page shows Open Mail to check access.
- **Mailboxes.** In the step Choose mailboxes and start reading, tick the mailboxes Projects reads. Inbox (all accounts) and Sent (all accounts) are ticked until you change them, and the mailboxes of each account and those On My Mac are under their name. Period sets how far back it reads: the last 30, 90, 180 or 365 days, 90 unless you change it. Start Reading Mail stays off until Mail access is allowed and a mailbox is ticked, and the line beside it says which is missing. No mail is read before you click Start Reading Mail, which also opens the Projects window. After that, the same list shows under Mailboxes, and a change of the mailboxes or the period counts at once for the mail Projects reads from then on. Mail it already saved stays until it expires or you delete Projects data. A mailbox you link to a project is listed there too, with the project's name and Edit Project…, since Projects reads it as well, ticked or not. While Mail is open, Projects looks for new mail every minute.
- **The Projects window.** Open Projects Window opens it: in Settings, Projects (in the last step of Set Up Projects until you click Start Reading Mail, at the top after that), in Orra's menu in the menu bar, where it also says how many emails wait for a reply, and in the menu of Orra's Dock icon. On the left are Needs a Project, Set Aside, the projects Orra suggests and your projects. In the middle is the mail of the one you chose, and on the right the email you select, or the project's page. With no email selected and no project's page, the right side shows How to Use Projects: the steps from choosing mailboxes to a reply in Mail, with New Project… to make a project. The step about the mailboxes, with Open Projects Settings… and, while Mail is closed, Open Mail, shows until you click Start Reading Mail. While the window is open, Orra shows in the Dock and keeps the cleanup model loaded. Closing the window lets it go. Under the mail list, or in its middle while the list is empty, a line says what Projects is doing, such as Reading mail… with the number of emails read so far, or Sorting mail… while it sorts mail. It also says why no mail comes in: no mailbox is chosen yet, Mail is not open, Mail is slow to answer, Orra is not allowed to read Mail, or some mail could not be read. Where it helps, Try Again, Settings… or Open Mail comes with the line. While the cleanup model is not on this Mac, a line under the list says so, with Settings…, or shows the download's progress while it downloads. A list shows its newest 1,000 emails, and when it holds more, a line under it says how many, with Load Older Emails. Explain Message Selected in Mail in the toolbar shows and explains the message you selected in Mail, when it is in a mailbox Projects reads, within the period it reads. It must be the email Projects read there, with the same sender, recipients and date.
- **Projects and folders.** Click New Project… under the list. Give it a name and a description: what the project is, who the other side is and which language they write in. Add its folders with Add Folder…, or drag them in. Projects reads the text of the PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, RTF, OpenDocument, Markdown, plain text and CSV files in them, and recognizes the text of images and of scanned PDF pages on this Mac, up to 5,000 files a project, documents before images, and up to 100 scanned pages a file. It never changes a file. Text recognition reads Chinese and English. In other languages it reads Latin letters without their accents, and Japanese only in part. Recognizing text takes longer than reading it, so it comes after the other files are read, and it waits as Dictation goes first describes. It knows every other file there by its name and folder, such as a drawing, an archive or a video, up to 20,000 more files a project. It skips files that are not on this Mac, such as iCloud Drive files that are not downloaded, and never reads your home folder, a whole disk or Mail's local data files. Add the people of the project with their email address and role, the domains of the companies you work with, such as harbor.example, and a Mail mailbox linked to the project, whose mail always goes to it. Every address at such a domain counts as one of the project's people. Its mail goes to the project, and a reply to it counts as staying inside the project. Add a person at a mail provider many people share, such as gmail.com, under People instead, since New Project and Edit Project do not take such a domain. The project's page shows how many files Projects read, how many it finds by name only, how many images and scans wait for text recognition or why it waits, which it skipped and why, and the files that hide text from their reader, whose hidden text it leaves out. Edit Project… changes it.
- **Sorting and rules.** Projects files a new email in the project of its thread, of a person, a domain or a rule you set, or of a linked mailbox. When these leave it open, the model chooses while the Projects window is open, and its choice shows Unsure project. Mail no project fits goes to Needs a Project. Newsletters and other mail sent to a list or by a machine go to Set Aside, unless one of your projects' people is in them. The chip at the top of an email says why it is there. A click on it assigns the email to another project, as Assign to Project in the context menu of the list does, and the email stays where it is in Mail. Its explanation and draft go, and you can explain it again. When you changed the draft, Orra asks first. After you assign two emails from one sender or one domain to the same project, Orra offers a rule that assigns such mail to it from then on. The project's page lists its rules, each with Remove Rule…, which stops the rule for new mail and leaves the mail it assigned in the project. After you add, edit or forget a project, or add a rule, Projects sorts again the mail the change concerns: the mail in Needs a Project, the mail marked Unsure project whose text Projects still keeps, the mail of the people, domains and mailboxes you added or removed, and the mail of the sender or domain of a rule you added. Replies assigned to a project through the threads of that mail are sorted again too. When Projects sorts again, mail you assigned yourself, an email with a draft and mail a rule of yours assigned stay where they are. In the chip, “Someone in it wrote in the project before” rests on mail you assigned to the project or that Projects assigned there through the project's people, domains, rules or linked mailboxes. Replies assigned to the project through the threads of that mail count too. The model's choice alone does not count. Needs reply marks mail that waits for your answer, and an explained email can show its deadline.
- **Suggested projects.** Below Needs a Project and Set Aside, the sidebar lists Suggested Projects, each with the number of emails it would take. Orra groups the mail of Needs a Project by thread and by the other side: the domain of a company, a person at a shared provider such as gmail.com, or a Mail mailbox you file mail into. Threads left over are grouped by a word their subjects share, such as the name of an event. It leaves out mail you put in Needs a Project yourself, mailing list mail and mail a project already covers, and shows at most 8 groups of 3 emails or more. While the window is open, the model names each group and describes it in a line, in the language of its mail. Click a suggestion, or choose Create Project… in its menu, to open New Project with the name, description, people, domains and mailbox filled in. Change what you like, add folders and save it. Or choose Dismiss Suggestion, there or in the suggestion's menu, and it does not come back. Orra makes no project without your click, and sorts the mail again once you save one.
- **Explaining an email.** Before you explain an email, the window shows the email itself, as Mail does: To and Cc, its attachments with whether Projects read their text, the sender's words, each forwarded message under its own header, and the quoted text folded under Quoted Text. Click Explain, and the model first turns the email into search terms in Chinese and English, with other words for the same things, so a file in English can match an email in Chinese. Orra finds the passages of the project's files that match the email's words or these terms, and of the files you chose for the email, and the model reads the email, its thread, the project's other mail, the text of its attachments and those passages. An email in Needs a Project gets the passages of all your projects' folders that match at least three of its search terms, and an email in a project also gets up to 2 passages of your other projects' folders that match at least two of its search terms and that the search ranks above every passage of its own project that matches one of them, after the project's own passages. An email the checks warn about, such as about its sender, hidden text, payment details or a request for personal data or a password, gets none from other projects, and mail in Set Aside gets no files. Explain Again uses the same search terms. The explanation is in Chinese when Orra's language is Chinese, and in English otherwise. Explain Again explains it anew. When you changed the draft, Explain Again, and Explain Message Selected in Mail for an email explained before, ask Replace your draft? first, and only Replace Draft replaces it. After Explain, the explanation comes first, then the draft, and the email itself follows under Original Email. Hide Explanation, beside Explain Again, shows the email first with the draft under it, for every email until you close the window, and Show Explanation brings the explanation back. Compare with Original, beside them, shows the email next to its explanation and draft. The sidebar hides, and the mail list stays. Original Email holds the email itself, with the sender's words first and its recipients and attachments folded under them, and Explanation and Draft holds the explanation and the draft. Each scrolls on its own, side by side in a wide window and one above the other in a narrow one, and the draft keeps what you typed. Another email you choose in the list shows the same way. Exit Comparison, in the bar above them, shows the sidebar as it was. Choosing another list or closing the window also ends the comparison. Explain and Explain Again show the explanation they make. Without the cleanup model, Explain shows a line that says so, and Open Projects Settings… beside it leads to the download. When the model is on your Mac but could not be loaded, the line says so, with Try Loading Again. An email over 16 MB, which Projects does not read, and an email whose text Projects removed after 90 days show a line that says so, and Explain stays off. Read those in Mail. The explanation shows:
  - **Warnings**, such as text its reader cannot see, a sender's domain that looks like one Projects knows but is a different one, or a link. Orra's checks look at the words, so a warning about a password or a payment says that the email mentions it, not that it asks for it. The warnings of Orra's own checks show before you click Explain too.
  - **What it says**, the points of the email. Each point has chips for its sources, or says No source provided. The chip of a file shows the excerpt saved with the explanation, with its page, slide or sheet, and the chip of an attachment the part of it the model read, both inside Orra. The This email chip shows the whole email, with its recipients and attachments folded at the end. Lines listed under Taken out before the model read it stay in a saved excerpt, though the model never read them. An excerpt recognized in an image or a scanned page says so, since its words and numbers may be wrong. The chip of another project's file names that project in brackets, and a line under the points says that such files are for you only and, when the explanation has a draft, that the draft was written without them. The chip of an earlier email shows that email in the window, and Back to Previous Email above it takes you back. Nothing opens outside Orra. When Projects could not read an attachment of a kind it reads, such as a PDF that Mail has not downloaded, a line under the points says so.
  - **Project Files for This Explanation**, folded under What it says, with the number of files. Open, it lists each project file whose excerpts Projects gave the model for this explanation: its name, its folder inside the project folder that holds it, Cited in explanation or Not cited in explanation, and why it was included: Chosen by you, Found by search, or One of the project's newest files, added because search found few matches, when search found fewer than three excerpts. A file from the folders of another project says so, with that project's name. Each excerpt holds at most about 800 characters, so the model never reads a long file whole. Not cited does not mean that the model ignored a file. A chip such as Page 2, Slide 3, Sheet “Budget” or Saved Excerpt shows that excerpt inside Orra. A file whose folder left the project says Not in the project's folders now, and a file in a project folder that is not available, such as one on a disk that is not connected, says Its project folder is not available now. Edit Project… changes the project's folders, and a change counts from the next Explain Again, once indexing finishes. For an email in no project, Assign to Project takes its place. Explanations made before Orra 0.6.1 do not say why a file was included.
  - **Before you reply**, the questions only you can decide, or No decisions found. When the model did not finish a part, that part says so, and Explain Again tries once more.
- **Choosing files for an email.** Orra finds the passages of the project's files that fit an email on its own. To point it at files yourself, click Choose Files for This Email… under Project Files for This Explanation, or choose it in the email's context menu in the list, also before the first Explain. It lists the files of your project folders, the email's project first and then the other projects under their names, each with its folder, its kind, whether Projects can read its text, and its date. Search by File Name finds a file in every project. Tick up to 6 files and click Explain Again, or Save to explain later. Orra then puts the passages of those files that best fit the email first, or the beginning of a file where none fits, and fills the rest of its 6 passages with its own search. Of a file without text, such as a drawing, the model gets only the name. Until the next explanation, a line under Explain or Explain Again says that it uses the files you chose. Project Files for This Explanation then opens and shows them first, Chosen by you, and a chosen file that gave the model nothing, such as one Projects has not read, last with the reason. Return to Automatic Selection lets Orra choose again. A file outside your project folders cannot be chosen. Drag it onto the list, and Add Its Folder to the project opens that folder in an open panel, for you to add it to the project for all its mail. The file is then ticked, and the model gets it once Projects has read the folder. The choice is kept with the email's text, 90 days, and a file leaves it when you forget the project of its folder.
- **The draft.** When the email needs a reply, a draft follows, in the email's language, Chinese or English. An email that Projects finds to be in another language, such as Japanese, gets no draft, and mail you sent or answered later in its thread, or that came from a list or a machine, needs no reply. Drafts need checking, so read every line. Orra takes out links, sentences that claim something was done or that speak as an assistant, and project data in a reply to someone outside the project. When an explanation reads files of another project, the model writes the draft in a second request without them, so the draft takes nothing from them, and such an explanation takes longer. Orra also takes out of every draft what it recognizes of another project's files, such as amounts, codes, mail addresses, the names of people and of that project, and copied sentences. It marks a date whose weekday is wrong and an amount no source holds. The answer check says how many of the email's questions the draft answers, such as “They asked 3 things, your draft answers 2”, and a reply to all names the people outside the project it reaches. You can edit the draft as text. Orra saves what you type, also when you close the window, and Saved on this Mac shows under the draft once it is saved. Tone rewrites it as Formal, Warm or Firm, and Show in Chinese or Show in English shows its translation beside it.
- **Changing the draft, also by voice.** In the box under the draft, say what to change, such as “more formal, keep the price, delivery on November 22”, and click Change Draft. The new version shows its changes highlighted, and Undo Change brings back the draft as it was before, with what you typed, also in a window you open later. Cancel Change ends a change and leaves the draft as it was. While a change waits, such as during a dictation, a line says why, and Copy copies the draft as it is. With the cursor in that box, the talk key's words go straight into it, without the clipboard, and when you finish speaking, the draft changes. To read your words first and click Change Draft yourself, turn off Change the draft when you finish speaking in Settings, Projects. The translation key works in the draft as in any app. The box changes the draft only. It opens no file you name in it. When you name one, a line under the box offers Choose Files for This Email…
- **Replying.** Open Reply in Mail, or Reply All when the email went to more people, opens Mail's reply window with the quote and your signature, puts the draft on the clipboard and brings Mail to the front. Press Command V there, check the reply and send it yourself. Copy only puts the draft on the clipboard. There is no Send button, and Orra never sends mail. As with dictated text, the draft stays on this Mac's clipboard.
- **Dictation goes first.** While you dictate, while Live Captions run, while the Mac is low on memory, and on a Mac with less than 24 GB of memory while the voice of Speak Translations to Others is loaded, Projects pauses its work on the model, and a line under the mail list says why. With the window closed, new mail is sorted by the rules only, and the model sorts the rest once you open the window again. A load of the cleanup model that only Projects needs also waits until the dictation ends. Recognizing text in the images and scans of project folders waits too, page by page: while you dictate, while Live Captions run, while you wait for an explanation or a change of the draft, while the Mac is low on memory or hot, and while Low Power Mode is on. The project's page says when it waits for memory, heat or Low Power Mode. A press of the talk key or the translation key stops the page under way, which is read again later.
- **Turning it off and deleting.** Turning Projects off in Settings asks whether to keep what it keeps for the next time or delete it. Forget Project…, on the project's page or in its context menu, removes one project with its index, mail and drafts, and the explanations and drafts of other emails that used its files. Delete All Projects Data… in Settings, Projects deletes everything Projects keeps, your choice of mailboxes too. Stored on this Mac shows how much that is. Your mail in Mail and the files in your folders stay as they are. Assigning, editing and deleting in Projects change only what it keeps on this Mac, never your mail in Mail. The only change Projects asks of Mail is a new reply window, when you click Open Reply in Mail or Reply All. Mail may save that reply in Drafts, as it does any reply. Projects keeps the text of an email and its explanation for 90 days after reading it, and a draft with what you typed into it for 30 days after its last change, but never longer than the email's text. When the draft goes, the explanation stays, and Explain Again writes a new draft. Settings, Projects shows these periods under Data. It removes what is older when it starts and once a day while it is on, so what you keep while it is off stays until you turn it on again or delete it. [privacy.md](privacy.md) has the details.
